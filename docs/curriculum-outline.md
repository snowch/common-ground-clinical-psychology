# Curriculum and evidence map

## 1. Understand distress without turning it into a label

Distinguish distress, impairment and diagnosis, and identify a proportionate support response.

- Clinical psychology studies difficulties and care.
- A diagnosis is a professional judgment.
- Support does not require a label.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: What are psychological disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-1-what-are-psychological-disorders); [NICE NG197: Shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197)

## 2. Classification is a tool with limits

Explain what diagnostic categories organize and why dimensions and context still matter.

- Systems organize patterns of difficulty.
- Dimensions describe degree and variation.
- Reliability and validity are different questions.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Diagnosing and classifying psychological disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-2-diagnosing-and-classifying-psychological-disorders); [WHO ICD-11: International Classification of Diseases](https://icd.who.int/browse/2026-01/mms/en)

## 3. Assessment gathers evidence; formulation connects it

Distinguish a screening score, clinical assessment and a revisable formulation.

- A screen is not a diagnosis.
- Assessment integrates several sources.
- A formulation is a testable working account.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Diagnosing and classifying psychological disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-2-diagnosing-and-classifying-psychological-disorders); [NICE NG197: Shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197); [NICE NG222: Depression in adults: treatment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)

## 4. Anxiety can protect and constrain

Explain how threat and avoidance may interact without diagnosing from ordinary worry.

- Fear and anxiety are not automatically disorders.
- Avoidance can reduce distress immediately.
- Treatment is assessed and collaborative.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Anxiety disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-4-anxiety-disorders); [NICE CG113: Generalised anxiety disorder and panic disorder in adults: management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg113)

## 5. Low mood has several possible contexts

Distinguish a depressive syndrome from one low mood and explain why assessment and support matter.

- A mood is not the whole pattern.
- A maintaining process is not a blame statement.
- Care choices depend on assessed need.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Mood disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-7-mood-disorders); [NICE NG222: Depression in adults: treatment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)

## 6. Trauma exposure and diagnosis are different

Explain why trauma responses vary and why supportive care should not force disclosure.

- Exposure does not dictate one response.
- Reminders can have learned significance.
- Safety and choice come first.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Posttraumatic stress disorder](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-6-posttraumatic-stress-disorder); [NICE NG116: Post-traumatic stress disorder](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng116)

## 7. Unusual experiences need careful assessment

Distinguish broad symptom domains from diagnoses and avoid stereotypes about danger or identity.

- Psychosis describes a domain of experience.
- Bipolar disorder is not ordinary variability.
- Respectful support avoids confrontation and stereotypes.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Schizophrenia](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-8-schizophrenia); [NICE CG178: Psychosis and schizophrenia in adults: prevention and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg178); [NICE CG185: Bipolar disorder: assessment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185)

## 8. Therapies make different kinds of claims

Compare treatment approaches and separate theoretical rationale from evidence for a specified use.

- A therapy model guides a treatment.
- Approaches emphasize different processes.
- Care should be informed and reviewable.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Types of treatment](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-2-types-of-treatment); [NICE NG222: Depression in adults: treatment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222); [NICE NG197: Shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197)

## 9. Treatment claims need fair comparisons

Interpret average treatment effects using comparison groups, absolute outcomes, uncertainty and harms.

- Randomization supports a specific inference.
- Absolute outcomes make the claim interpretable.
- Benefits and harms need the same attention.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Treatment modalities](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-3-treatment-modalities); [NICE NG222: Depression in adults: treatment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222); [NICE NG197: Shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197)

## 10. An informed next step is a useful outcome

Build a proportionate response that combines evidence, preferences, access and appropriate professional boundaries.

- A care route should match the concern.
- Prepare questions without self-diagnosing.
- Keep the explanation revisable.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Mental health treatment past and present](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-1-mental-health-treatment-past-and-present); [NICE NG197: Shared decision making](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197); [NICE NG222: Depression in adults: treatment and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)
